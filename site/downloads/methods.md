# Methods and interpretation

This first milestone tests whether current public CMS hospital data can support a Wisconsin hospital-level association between discharge information and 30-day hospital-wide readmissions. The protocol was committed locally as `7bbcf60` before downloading hospital outcome data. That local Git timestamp is an audit trail, not external preregistration. No outcome model has been fit.

We retrieved the current CMS catalog metadata for patient survey (dgck-syfz; released August 13, 2026), general information (xubh-q36u; August 13, 2026), and unplanned visits (632h-zaca; September 30, 2026) on October 29, 2026. “Current” refers to these catalog snapshots. The HCAHPS file remains the August release at retrieval; the public reporting timetable alone is not evidence that October values were available there. Full bytes and dictionaries are retained locally. The repository includes a bounded reproducible extract, source manifest, national integrity audit, official date table and footnote crosswalk. National files total about 126 MB and are deliberately excluded from Git; `restore_sources.py` restores exact source bytes and rejects changed hashes.

National facility IDs and facility/measure keys were audited before extracting Wisconsin. There are no duplicate general-information IDs or facility/measure keys in the three national files. The national HCAHPS and unplanned-visit files each include 4,790 facilities. General information includes 5,419. In Wisconsin, general information includes 140 facilities: 64 acute care, 58 critical access, 13 psychiatric, three VA and two children's hospitals. We restrict to the 64 acute-care hospitals for structural comparability; this excludes much of rural Wisconsin and does not make the remaining hospitals equivalent.

The preselected exposure is H_COMP_6_Y_P, the adjusted percentage answering yes to the discharge-information composite: discussion of help after discharge and written information about symptoms/problems to watch for. It does not isolate handout quality, comprehension, nursing work or a specific intervention. All 64 acute-care hospitals have scores, ranging from 81 to 94 (median 89). One has 83 completed surveys and footnote 6; it is excluded from the primary exposure subset. The remaining 63 also have median 89. With >=300 surveys, 52 remain (median 89; range 83–94). These are descriptive sensitivity checks of the exposure, not readmission results. Hospital survey response rates range from 14% to 55% (median 30%); nonresponse bias remains possible. Completion counts are not item-specific denominators.

The claims-based READM_30_HOSP_WIDE ID is absent from this national release. The protocol anticipated auditing the separately named Hybrid_HWR replacement; we did so without substituting a condition-specific measure. Its Score, Denominator and interval bounds are Not Available for all 64 Wisconsin acute-care hospitals, with footnote 4 (suppressed by CMS for one or more quarters). Across the entire national file, all 4,790 Hybrid_HWR scores are nonnumeric: 4,478 carry footnote 4, 140 footnote 5 and 172 footnote 19. This is an availability check, not geographic expansion of the analytical sample. The precise underlying suppression reason was not established by the crosswalk; we do not attribute it to hospital performance or invent values.

A second, independent barrier is time. Every selected HCAHPS record covers October 1, 2024–September 30, 2025, while Hybrid_HWR covers July 1, 2024–June 30, 2025. Their inclusive intersection is 273 days, but neither 365-day period equals the other. None passes the exact-date rule. The funnel reaches zero at the numeric outcome stage; the final zero removed by the date stage does not imply dates matched. All 64 fail that independent check. Merely relaxing temporal eligibility would still yield zero numeric pairs.

HCAHPS changed with January 2025 discharges: Care Transition was removed; new content, web-first modes, proxy responses and a longer collection period were introduced. Discharge Information is one of the eight unchanged measures carried through transition, but this window spans pre/post changes in survey administration. We use one release only and do not claim a clean comparison across 2025 or interchange Care Transition, Care Coordination and Discharge Information. HCAHPS samples adults across payers; hospital-wide Medicare readmission reporting concerns a different population. Matching periods would not resolve that population mismatch, ecological bias, case-mix and social-risk differences, hospital resources, postacute access or response timing/reverse causation.

The protocol permits a single expansion to WI/IL/IA/MI/MN when fewer than 60 eligible Wisconsin hospitals remain and dataset periods are compatible. It explicitly forbids expanding to repair dataset-level time mismatch. Expansion was not triggered. No correlation, slope, p-value, causal effect, financial scenario or savings claim is reported. The absence of an estimable association is not evidence of no association.

Next: audit a pinned historical release with unsuppressed hospital-wide scores and aligned HCAHPS dates; document a protocol amendment before viewing historical associations. Recheck definitions, Medicare population and hybrid/claims comparability, selection and suppression. Use the predeclared five-state expansion only after temporal and numeric feasibility passes. If valid matched data remain unavailable, retain this feasibility finding and design a separate prospective study with appropriate partners; no recruitment or patient records were collected here.
